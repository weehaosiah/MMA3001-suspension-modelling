## Rubric review: what distinguishes the four levels

The project is worth 25% of the unit mark (p1). Pages10-15 contain six criteria,
each with Excellent, Good, Satisfactory and Poor descriptions. They provide no
numeric category weights, grade boundaries or equal-weighting rule. Page8
emphasises the presentation and answers about the work; it does not provide a
separate report/repository/presentation percentage split. The summaries below
paraphrase the rubric; they are not marks assigned to this project.

| Criterion | Excellent | Good | Satisfactory | Poor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Engineering problem, inputs and outputs (p10) | Precise, complete context and purpose; justified scope/limits; formats, units, domains and meaning fully specified. | Clear problem and most inputs/outputs, with minor gaps. | Basic explanation; vague purpose or important missing input/output details. | Missing/unclear problem; incorrect or undefined inputs/outputs and context. |
| GitHub structure, commits and README (p11) | Logical, consistent, fully reproducible organisation; complete orientation/workflow/dependency/data guidance; insightful commits and/or branching. | Well organised, meaningful history and mostly complete instructions, with minor gaps. | Inconsistent organisation or incomplete README; reproduction needs assistance. | Disorganised or unreproducible; unusable README or missing dependencies/data. |
| Comments, docstrings and HTML (p12) | Comments explain intent; consistent function documentation covers parameters, returns, exceptions and usage; complete HTML remains in sync with code. | Helpful documentation across most functions/modules; minor omissions or outdated sections. | Sporadic/literal comments, incomplete docstrings and minimal HTML. | Missing, misleading or incorrect documentation; broken HTML; code requires outside explanation. |
| Validation and engineering credibility (p13) | Rigorous, appropriate design, correct metrics/results, credible comparisons, failure/unsupported-input identification and honest limitations. | Appropriate, mostly complete validation; some aspects unexplored, with limits acknowledged. | Superficial checks, weak metrics/comparisons and limited discussion of limits. | Missing/incorrect validation, misleading results or absent comparisons/limits. |
| Justification of computational approach (p14) | Deep reasoning, credible baseline, quantitative evidence, supported improvements and thoughtful trade-offs. | Solid reasoning, some quantitative comparison/optimisation and trade-off discussion. | Weak baseline/justification, little quantitative analysis or speculative improvements. | No meaningful comparison, performance analysis or supported justification. |
| AI use and critical reflection (p15) | Full disclosure and critical evaluation; verified understanding, errors recognised; student clearly directs the work and retains intellectual responsibility. | Disclosed, well managed, adequately reflected upon; understanding and student responsibility evident. | Disclosed but weakly justified/checked; superficial reflection or uncertain ownership. | Undisclosed/inappropriate use, unverified reliance, unrecognised errors or unclear understanding. |

A docstring is the explanatory text attached to a function in the code. It should
tell another person what to supply, what comes back and what can go wrong.
Reproducibility means another person can follow the recorded steps and obtain the
claimed result. Verification asks whether the equations/method were implemented
correctly; validation asks what evidence supports using the result for its intended
purpose. Our checks support the numerical model and predictions of its outputs;
they do not establish real-vehicle accuracy.
